# Respite plaza: Blender handoff

Build the five destination buildings from their individual studies, then assemble them around the
court in the selected composition. Respite is an inhabited human town inside immense older
machinery. The intended result is a stylized game scene with believable volumes, broad scuffed
textures, and strong atmosphere. The selected composition establishes the visual direction; the
individual studies define the buildings.

## World and habitation

Read the included [world canon](references/context/core-themes-world-fiction.md) before the build.
Respite, officially Habitat Nine, is the largest known human center. Its inhabitants maintain their
surroundings within infrastructure that extends far beyond their control. The world feels
artificial, luminous, polluted, overbuilt, and old.

Express habitation through usable entrances, maintained paths, sparse windows, and local light. Keep
people, crowds, humanoid robots, and animals out of the renders. The town has colour and care; it
does not read as an abandoned wasteland. Use sparse inherited pipes and machinery around the
architecture. Avoid total mechanical infestation.

## Court and camera

The exit is the primary gameplay destination. Give its circular mouth the strongest focal contrast
and an unobstructed, short approach. The stash is a frequent interaction and needs immediate
visibility. Keep the Codex, workshop, and bazaar individually recognizable at the final game camera
size.

Place the buildings around one court. Rotate their entrances toward that court instead of pointing
every facade at the camera. The bazaar entrance faces the stash across the court. An elevated
oblique camera can reveal the bazaar interior while its near wall hides some stalls. Preserve the
wall rather than removing it for visibility.

Keep the foreground as continuous plaza ground. Avoid a foreground retaining wall, railing, cliff,
or roof that draws attention below the scene. Fit the stash and bazaar within the frame. Preserve a
clear view of the workshop entrance behind the bazaar.

The habitat wall continues beyond both the left and right edges, with solid structure on both sides
of the tunnel. Its top sits slightly above the arch. Enormous curved machinery and service
structures rise behind the wall and extend beyond the top of the frame. Background machinery remains
subordinate to the destinations. The center circle in the composition is optional paving, not a
sixth interaction or raised platform.

## Building forms

### Codex

Use the compact two-storey body with deep rectangular rooms and mirrored angled front plates.
Preserve the recessed entrance and lilac slit. Use opaque sheened glass. The adjusted facade
reference establishes the mirrored front panels; the first pass uses paired lower-front windows
beneath them and one small upper-side window. Keep the long side mostly solid. Avoid an office
window grid or the tall tower from the atmosphere references.

### Workshop

Use one short boiler-like drum with a horizontal amber process slit. Retain the thick purposeful
pipes and compact service entrance. Extend the building with its rectangular rear hall. Do not
extend its depth through repeated cylindrical blocks. One modest service-room window is enough.
Convey advanced fabrication through the machinery, without visible hand-tool displays or detailed
workbenches.

### Bazaar

Use right option 02: a broad horseshoe court with three substantial roofed vendor wings. Each wing
has believable room depth. Retain both short entrance return walls and the banner gateway between
them. Keep the passage beneath the banner open.

Place supported purple fabric awnings over inward-facing vendor bays. Use shutters, broad counters,
and a few integral interfaces to suggest trade. Avoid individual tins, jars, products, or shelf
arrangements. The bazaar must read as several vendors around a shared space, rather than a
convenience store. Use consistent rectangular volumes and plausible canopy supports.

### Stash

Use the deep reinforced vault with one thick main door leaf partly open. The main vault mouth is the
entrance. Preserve the large hinges and small integrated control trim. Keep the vestibule dark and
empty, with a short turn that limits the interior view. Do not add a separate service-door annex.

### Exit

Retain the circular mouth, observation tower on its right, sparse sheened windows, and soft cyan
inner rim. Integrate the mouth into the immense habitat wall. The wall supplies the passage depth;
discard the standalone long host body from the asset study. Let the tunnel continue into fog. Keep
its floor flush with the approach, without a long bridge.

## Materials and detail

Use [texture_ref.png](references/texture_ref.png) for broad painted scuffs, soft material variation,
and gentle bevels. Its orange palette and robots are outside this scene's direction. Concentrate
modeled detail in silhouettes, openings, panels, and major pipes. Keep ordinary door sizes
consistent across the buildings before adjusting their overall scale.

Use mostly charcoal and neutral painted metal. Apply surviving coloured paint sparingly. Avoid
repetitive orange-and-teal panels that make the buildings resemble a toy set. Broad matte wear must
remain legible from the game camera. Avoid dense fine scratches, noisy paving, glossy wet ground,
and excessive props.

Keep the buildings substantial and habitable. Their depth must support the rooms implied by their
doors. Hidden geometry can remain economical, but visible side walls and roof depth must be
believable.

## Light and fog

Use [buildings_pop.png](references/buildings_pop.png) and [backdrop.png](references/backdrop.png)
for destination clarity, texture treatment, and atmospheric separation. Their building designs do
not override the individual studies.

Make the final render darker and foggier than composition 9d05. Preserve clear local light on the
destinations. Use navy, cobalt, blue, and purple through the environment, with selective cyan and
violet highlights. Colour comes mainly from light, with restrained local warmth.

| Destination | Light character                                     |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Exit        | Primary cyan rim and light within the passage       |
| Stash       | Modest warm-white entrance light                    |
| Codex       | Lilac accent and restrained entrance light          |
| Workshop    | Local amber process slit                            |
| Bazaar      | Purple accents and modest light beneath the awnings |

Give distant infrastructure lower contrast through layered fog. Keep stronger darkness between the
light sources. Avoid uniform purple haze across every object, repeated neon frames, and evenly
spaced amber lamps across the entire plaza. Retain visible foreground scuffs beneath the atmosphere.

## Build sequence

1. Inspect all references and the world canon.
2. Block out the five buildings and wall with consistent door scale.
3. Assemble the court and establish the elevated game camera.
4. Develop the approved silhouettes, broad materials, and local lights.
5. Add atmospheric depth and produce the final camera render.

Check the court at step 3 before detailed asset work. The exit must dominate, the stash must remain
obvious, and every destination must have a readable approach. Render geometry checks with little fog
so atmosphere cannot conceal missing walls or inconsistent volumes. Then use the darker, foggier
final treatment.

Keep each destination in its own named Blender collection. Keep the camera, wall, lights, and fog
separately editable. Save the editable scene with its textures and named materials. Exact
dimensions, polygon counts, camera lens, and export settings remain implementation choices; the
concept art does not specify them.

The included [game renderer document](references/context/game-rendering.md) describes the project
integration context. In-game panels remain separate from the spatial scene. A Blender beauty render
does not establish runtime compatibility. Check the actual export and material path before runtime
integration; retain an editable source scene.

## Completion checks

- The five destinations face the common court, with a clear exit and an unobscured stash.
- The habitat wall reaches both side edges around the tunnel, and the foreground remains open
  ground.
- The individual studies define the models, including one workshop drum and the complete bazaar
  enclosure.
- The scene is darker and foggier than 9d05, with distinct destinations and readable broad textures.
- The editable scene and final camera render contain no people or microscopic vendor inventory.
